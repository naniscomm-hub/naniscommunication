Beyond the Stage: The Creative World of Kim Nam-joon
BTS Leader · Artist · Songwriter · Art Collector

“Influence is not measured only by the size of an audience, but by the ideas, curiosity and cultural connections an artist inspires.”

Nanis Communication · 11 November 2026

More Than a Global Music Icon

Kim Nam-joon, known professionally as RM, has built a public identity that reaches well beyond the boundaries of popular music. As the leader of BTS, he has performed before audiences around the world. Away from the stage, his interests in literature, visual art, museums and reflective living reveal another dimension of his creative life.

His story offers a compelling view of contemporary cultural influence: an artist whose work connects music with questions of identity, heritage, creativity and well-being.

1. The ARIRANG Era: A Leader Back on the Global Stage

In March 2026, BTS returned as a complete group with “BTS Comeback Live: ARIRANG” at Seoul’s historic Gwanghwamun Square. The performance connected a major contemporary music event with one of Korea’s most recognisable cultural landmarks.

RM’s role as group leader was especially visible in the way he introduced the performance and spoke about the creative direction behind the project. The new album “ARIRANG” brought together the members’ reflections on identity, change and the experience of returning to music after an extended period apart.

For RM, leadership is not simply a position within a successful group. It is also the responsibility of communicating the group’s artistic intentions and helping its members’ voices reach an international audience.

2. RM x SFMOMA: A Personal Art Collection Enters the Museum

One of the most significant cultural developments in RM’s recent career is “RM x SFMOMA: Between You and Me”, presented by the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art from 3 October 2026 to 7 February 2027.

The exhibition brings together approximately 200 works from the collections of RM and SFMOMA. Nearly 150 works come from RM’s personal collection, assembled over almost a decade. The presentation places Korean modern and contemporary art in conversation with works from the museum’s collection and international artistic traditions.

This is RM’s first museum partnership as an exhibition co-curator. He worked with SFMOMA curators América Castillo and Hyoeun Kim, contributing to the selection and presentation of works. He also wrote personal reflections for gallery texts and recorded an audio guide, giving visitors insight into the art that has moved him.

The exhibition is not simply a display of celebrity-owned objects. It offers visitors an opportunity to encounter Korean art across different periods and mediums, including painting, sculpture, ceramics and works on paper. Many of the works are being shown publicly in the United States for the first time.

By sharing his collection, RM creates a meeting point between personal taste and public cultural experience. His approach invites visitors to discover art through emotional connection and curiosity, rather than through status or market value alone.

3. “Namjooning”: Everyday Curiosity as a Way of Living

The word “Namjooning” emerged among BTS fans to describe activities associated with RM’s interests: visiting museums, reading, cycling, spending time in nature and making room for reflection. It has become a shorthand for slowing down and finding inspiration beyond the demands of work and public life.

Its wider appeal lies in its simplicity. These are accessible activities, yet they can encourage observation, self-awareness and a more balanced relationship with everyday life.

4. Stanford University: A Course Inspired by BTS and Well-Being

RM’s cultural influence has also entered an academic setting. Stanford University’s 2026–2027 course catalogue lists PSYC 65N, “Namjooning, and Other Well-Being Inspiration from BTS”, taught by Associate Professor James R. Jacobs.

The introductory seminar uses BTS and the idea of “Namjooning” as an entry point for examining well-being. Topics listed by Stanford include nature and biking, art and literature, journaling, sleep, anxiety, creative blocks, self-awareness, exercise, nutrition, psychotherapy and coaching.

The course is an academic exploration inspired by the group and RM’s public interests. It does not mean RM is a Stanford lecturer or that he has received an academic appointment there. Its significance is that popular culture can provide a relatable starting point for evidence-based discussions about how people live and flourish.

“A museum, a book, a bicycle ride or a quiet moment outdoors can become an invitation to see the world differently.”

5. “Indigo”: Music as a Personal and Artistic Statement

Released in December 2022, RM’s solo album “Indigo” offered listeners a more intimate view of his artistic identity. The project moves across musical styles and collaborations while reflecting on memory, relationships, uncertainty and personal growth.

Its thoughtful presentation also reflects RM’s interest in visual art and the emotional connections between different creative disciplines. “Indigo” can be understood as more than a collection of songs: it is a record of an artist exploring who he is, what has shaped him and how he wishes to express himself.

6. Cultural Influence Beyond Entertainment

RM’s work with SFMOMA highlights the ability of artists to introduce audiences to cultural histories that may be unfamiliar to them. By placing Korean artists alongside works from different parts of the world, the exhibition encourages visitors to look for connections across geography, generations and artistic movements.

This is a form of cultural exchange rather than a formal diplomatic role. RM’s contribution comes through his platform as a musician, his personal collection and his willingness to share the art that has influenced him.

His example also challenges the idea that a successful entertainer must be defined by one discipline. Music, reading, art collecting and public communication can coexist, each enriching the others.

Conclusion: A Creative Legacy Still Taking Shape

Kim Nam-joon’s journey is a reminder that influence can develop in unexpected directions. From the global stages of BTS to the galleries of SFMOMA and the academic conversation at Stanford, his interests have created new ways for audiences to engage with music, art and well-being.

These developments do not replace his identity as a musician. They add depth to it. RM remains first and foremost an artist and the leader of BTS, while his curiosity continues to open doors to other creative and cultural worlds.

Beyond the stage. Beyond the spotlight.

His influence is found not only in the music people hear, but also in the curiosity that encourages them to read, explore, reflect and discover.
