PRESS STATEMENT · MALAY CONSULTATIVE COUNCIL (MPM)

PROPOSED RM4.3 BILLION SALE OF BOUSTEAD PLANTATIONS: NATIONAL INTEREST, BUMIPUTERA OWNERSHIP AND STRATEGIC NATIONAL ASSETS MUST TAKE PRIORITY!

23 NOVEMBER 2026

MPM URGES LTAT TO PROVIDE A COMPREHENSIVE EXPLANATION AND GIVE SERIOUS CONSIDERATION TO SD GUTHRIE AS AN ALTERNATIVE

The Malay Consultative Council (MPM) views with serious concern reports regarding the proposed acquisition of Boustead Plantations Berhad (BPlant) by Achi Jaya Plantations Sdn Bhd, reportedly valued at approximately RM4.3 billion.

This matter must not be viewed solely from the perspective of corporate profits, the offer price or the buyer's ability to complete the transaction.

Instead, the overriding priorities must be national interest, Bumiputera ownership, the interests of contributors to the Armed Forces Fund Board (LTAT), and the preservation of strategic national assets for future generations.

BPlant is not an ordinary company. It owns extensive plantation interests across Peninsular Malaysia and holds strategic value in the country's commodity sector.

Such assets must be evaluated not only according to their current market value, but also according to their long-term potential contribution to the national economy.

MPM stresses that any decision to dispose of a strategic asset of this nature must be undertaken with due diligence, transparency and full accountability.

1. WHY SELL BPLANT WHEN ITS PERFORMANCE IS SHOWING SIGNS OF RECOVERY?

Financial reports cited by DagangNews on 23 November 2026 indicate that BPlant recorded improvements in revenue and operating profits for FY2025, alongside a reduction in its total borrowings.

The question is straightforward: if the company's operational and financial position is showing positive developments, why does LTAT still intend to dispose of this plantation asset?

Does LTAT genuinely require immediate cash?

Is there an urgent strategic necessity to divest these plantation interests?

Has a proper comparison been undertaken between the one-off proceeds from the sale and the potential dividends, income and appreciation in asset value over the next five to ten years?

MPM calls on LTAT to explain the financial and strategic justification for this proposed disposal to its contributors and the public.

Strategic assets with the potential to generate long-term returns must not be surrendered merely in pursuit of short-term transaction gains.

2. WHO ARE THE ULTIMATE BENEFICIAL OWNERS OF ACHI JAYA?

This is one of the most important questions and requires an official explanation.

Media reports and concerns raised by several parties have indicated that 73.78 per cent of the shareholding interests in Achi Jaya are associated with two Hong Kong-based companies, namely Herolite Investment Ltd and Grand Shine Trading Ltd.

If these figures and structures are accurate, who are the ultimate beneficial owners behind these two companies?

What voting rights, share conversion rights or shareholders' agreements could influence the actual control of the company?

MPM is not concluding that foreign ownership automatically constitutes a legal violation or the loss of Bumiputera control.

However, in a transaction of this magnitude, LTAT must clearly explain who actually controls the acquiring company, where its capital originates, and who will ultimately have the authority to determine corporate decisions following the acquisition.

Bumiputera status on paper does not necessarily guarantee that beneficial ownership and decision-making control remain in Bumiputera hands.

This matter must be thoroughly examined before any transaction is finalised.

3. WHY HAS SD GUTHRIE NOT BEEN GIVEN GREATER CONSIDERATION?

MPM wishes to raise questions regarding the position of SD Guthrie Berhad and Tradewinds Plantation Berhad, which have also reportedly participated in the bidding process.

SD Guthrie has extensive experience in the plantation industry and is among the world's leading players in the palm oil sector.

If a local company possesses the operational capability, industry experience, financial strength and ownership structure necessary to safeguard national interests, why has such an option not been given due consideration?

MPM proposes that LTAT reassess SD Guthrie's offer transparently, taking into account:

  • The price and overall value of the offer.
  • Financial capability and certainty of payment.
  • The company's ownership and control structure.
  • Its capacity to manage and develop the plantations.
  • Guarantees to safeguard Bumiputera interests.
  • Long-term commitments to LTAT contributors and the Armed Forces community.

MPM believes SD Guthrie deserves serious consideration as an alternative if its proposal provides better value and stronger protection of national interests.

Decisions must not be based merely on the name of a company. The overall interests of the nation must be the determining factor.

4. SEVEN CRITICAL QUESTIONS THAT LTAT MUST ANSWER

MPM raises seven fundamental questions requiring official clarification from LTAT.

FIRST: TRANSPARENCY IN THE SELECTION OF THE BUYER

What criteria were used by LTAT to evaluate the competing offers? What was the weighting assigned to each criterion, and what are the actual advantages of Achi Jaya's offer compared with those of SD Guthrie and Tradewinds?

Can the independent advisers' evaluation reports be disclosed to stakeholders?

SECOND: BUMIPUTERA OWNERSHIP AND ACTUAL CONTROL

Has LTAT verified the ultimate beneficial ownership of Achi Jaya, including its Hong Kong shareholders and offshore corporate structure?

How will LTAT ensure that genuine Bumiputera control is preserved after the transaction?

THIRD: THE BUYER'S FINANCIAL CAPABILITY

Does Achi Jaya have binding financing commitments for a transaction worth billions of ringgit?

What proportion of the acquisition will be funded through its own capital, external borrowings and financing potentially secured against BPlant's assets?

LTAT must ensure that the transaction is not merely supported by an attractive offer price, but can genuinely be completed without compromising the assets being acquired.

FOURTH: GUARANTEES THAT PLANTATION LAND WILL NOT BE SOLD OFF ARBITRARILY

Will the buyer be permitted to sell, transfer or change the use of plantation land following the acquisition?

If so, what safeguards and restrictions will apply?

MPM calls for any disposal of strategic land assets to be subject to clear controls and approval mechanisms through legally binding and enforceable agreements.

We do not want plantation land to be sold off in the future merely to repay acquisition financing.

FIFTH: PROTECTION OF LTAT'S REMAINING 25 PER CENT STAKE

If LTAT retains a 25 per cent minority stake, what legal and corporate protections will be provided?

How can LTAT prevent the disposal of important assets, unreasonable increases in debt or related-party transactions that could erode the value of its investment?

SIXTH: INDEPENDENT AUDIT AND ACCOUNTABILITY

Is LTAT prepared to commission an independent review of the entire valuation process, buyer selection and transaction structure?

MPM calls for the key findings of such a review to be disclosed to stakeholders before the final agreement is signed, subject to applicable legal requirements and legitimate commercial confidentiality obligations.

SEVENTH: WHY HAVE THE LESSONS FROM THE 2023 KLK TRANSACTION NOT BEEN ADDRESSED?

The proposed acquisition of BPlant by Kuala Lumpur Kepong Berhad (KLK) in 2023 was terminated following objections and concerns regarding the ownership and control of strategic assets.

What policy changes and evaluation criteria were introduced following that experience?

Why must questions concerning ownership, control and Bumiputera interests arise once again in the current proposed transaction?

LTAT must clarify these matters to ensure that the confidence of its contributors and the public is not undermined.

5. TO THE PRIME MINISTER AND THE MINISTER OF DEFENCE: NATIONAL INTEREST MUST BE GREATER THAN THE VALUE OF THE TRANSACTION

MPM wishes to draw the attention of the Prime Minister and the Minister of Defence to the fact that this issue has implications far beyond the commercial sale and purchase of a plantation company.

LTAT is an institution responsible for managing the savings and retirement benefits of eligible members of the Malaysian Armed Forces.

Therefore, the interests of serving military personnel, veterans, retirees and their families must remain the primary consideration.

We urge the Government to ensure that this process fully upholds the principles of good governance, contributors' interests and national strategic priorities.

We further call for no final decision to be made until all material questions concerning beneficial ownership, financing capability, land valuation and asset protection have been adequately answered.

This does not mean rejecting foreign investment altogether. Malaysia remains open to international investment that brings benefits to the country.

However, when strategic assets involving contributors' interests and Bumiputera ownership are at stake, actual control and Malaysia's long-term interests must receive due consideration.

6. DO NOT REPEAT PAST MISTAKES IN DISPOSING OF NATIONAL ASSETS

MPM wishes to issue an important reminder.

Assets that have been sold and transferred into other hands are not necessarily easy to reacquire.

We have witnessed various business assets and strategic interests that were once under local ownership eventually changing hands.

Once control has been transferred, there is no guarantee that such assets can be bought back in the future.

That is precisely why today's decisions must take into account the interests of future generations.

We cannot simply regard RM4.3 billion as a successful sale transaction.

We must ask:

  • What is the actual value of the assets being relinquished?
  • What future income and potential returns will be lost?
  • Who will ultimately control the land and plantation operations?
  • What guarantees exist that Bumiputera and national interests will continue to be protected?

WE MUST NOT SECURE SALE PROCEEDS TODAY ONLY TO LOSE A STRATEGIC NATIONAL ASSET FOREVER.

7. MPM'S PROPOSALS: DEFER FINALISATION AND REASSESS THE BEST OPTION

MPM puts forward the following proposals:

  1. LTAT should defer the finalisation of the transaction until all critical questions have been formally answered.
  2. An independent review should be conducted into the bidding process, valuation, ownership structure and the buyer's financial capability.
  3. The ultimate beneficial ownership structure and actual control rights of the buyer must be thoroughly verified.
  4. LTAT must ensure that BPlant's plantation land is protected through enforceable restrictions and agreements.
  5. The offers from SD Guthrie and Tradewinds should be objectively reassessed based on commercial value and national strategic interests.
  6. The Government must ensure that any final decision does not prejudice the interests of LTAT contributors, members of the Armed Forces, veterans and Bumiputera communities.
  7. The outcome of the evaluation and the justification for the decision should be transparently explained to stakeholders, subject to applicable regulations and disclosure obligations.

CONCLUSION: NATIONAL LAND AND STRATEGIC ASSETS ARE NOT MERE COMMODITIES FOR TRADE

MPM does not reject business principles, open competition or the need to secure the best possible returns from investments.

However, commercial profit cannot be the sole measure when decisions involve strategic assets directly connected to contributors' interests, Bumiputera ownership and the future of the nation.

We want LTAT to obtain the best possible value. We want contributors to be protected. We want the plantation industry to continue growing, and we want capable local companies to be given a fair opportunity.

If SD Guthrie can offer competitive value, strong financial guarantees and better protection of national interests, its proposal deserves serious consideration.

This is not about partisan politics. It is about trust, national interest and the future of generations of Malaysians.

Thank you.

DO NOT SELL STRATEGIC NATIONAL ASSETS WITHOUT STRONG GUARANTEES THAT BUMIPUTERA INTERESTS, LTAT CONTRIBUTORS AND MALAYSIA'S LONG-TERM INTERESTS WILL REMAIN PROTECTED.

NATIONAL ASSETS MUST BE MANAGED WITH INTEGRITY. BUMIPUTERA INTERESTS MUST BE PROTECTED. CONTRIBUTORS' INTERESTS MUST COME FIRST.

SHAHBUDIN EMBUN
Chairman, Political Bureau for Leadership and National Administration
Malay Consultative Council (MPM)
